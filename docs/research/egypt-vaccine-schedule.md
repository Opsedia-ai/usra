# Egypt childhood immunisation schedule (research notes)

Date researched: 2026-11-04

> Informational only; the child's health office card and the child's doctor are authoritative.

## Summary

- **Government (MoHP) mandatory schedule, birth to 18 months, is free at health offices and units.** It covers
  HepB birth dose, BCG, OPV (7 doses), IPV, pentavalent (DTwP-HepB-Hib), MMR (2 doses) and a DTP booster.
- **IPV is 3 doses (2, 4 and 6 months).** A single IPV dose at 4 months started on 22 July 2018. A
  MoHP letter changed this to 3 doses at 2, 4 and 6 months from February 2021. WHO/UNICEF coverage
  estimates (2025 revision, July 2026) record the "third dose of IPV introduced in 2021" and report IPVC coverage
  of 98% for 2025. Some news articles, including one from Dec 2024, still show only the old 4-month dose (see Disagreements).
- **There is a 9-month OPV dose (OPV4).** Our current data is confirmed for every item.
- **Hexavalent is NOT in the public (free) schedule as of 2026-11-04.** VACSERA sells it at its own paid
  centres (2, 4, 6 months plus an 18-month booster; electronic payment only, June 2026). I found no MoHP
  announcement that replaces pentavalent and IPV with hexavalent.
- **PCV and rotavirus are NOT in the public schedule.**
  - A Jan 2026 parliamentary request asked to add them, and a June 2026 report still calls both non-free.
  - The WHO/UNICEF 2025 revision has no PCV or rotavirus series for Egypt.
  - On 26 Oct 2026 the Health Minister described adding PCV to routine immunisation "before 2030" as a priority,
    tied to local PCV13 production (VBC with Walvax). That is a plan, not a schedule change.
- **School age:** MoHP gives bivalent meningococcal A+C to students in the first grade of each school
  stage (statement of 30 Mar 2026). A DT dose is given to primary-school pupils (2020 report), but I
  could not confirm the exact grade or age for it.

## Table B: Private hexavalent route

The model is "route = government" or "route = private-hexavalent". The private route swaps only the DTP-containing and IPV doses. Everything else stays on the government route.

| Age (months) | Private hexavalent dose | Replaces government doses | Still given from Table A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | none | none | HepB 0, OPV 0, BCG |
| 2 | Hexavalent 1 (DTaP-IPV-HepB-Hib, e.g. Hexyon / Infanrix hexa) | Pentavalent 1 + IPV 1 | OPV 1 |
| 4 | Hexavalent 2 | Pentavalent 2 + IPV 2 | OPV 2 |
| 6 | Hexavalent 3 | Pentavalent 3 + IPV 3 | OPV 3 |
| 9 | none | none | OPV 4 |
| 12 | none | none | OPV 5, MMR 1 |
| 18 | Hexavalent booster | DTP booster (and adds HepB/Hib/IPV boosters) | OPV 6, MMR 2 |

Notes:
- **Hexavalent ages:** VACSERA (Youm7, 21 Jun 2026) gives 2, 4 and 6 months plus a booster at 18 months.
- **OPV still applies on this route.** Egypt runs all routine polio doses and national campaigns (birth to 5 years) as OPV for every child. No source says a hexavalent child is exempt from the health-office OPV doses or from the MMR doses.
  - The app should keep all OPV and MMR doses on both routes.
  - It should mark the overlap ("IPV already covered by hexavalent; OPV still given at health office") as needing doctor confirmation.
- Some private paediatricians give a pentavalent or DTaP booster at 18 m instead of a hexavalent booster. Record whichever product was actually given.

## Table C: Optional (private) vaccines, one default schedule each

Ages are in months. The defaults follow common Egyptian private practice and the product labelling. Where an Egyptian source differs, the difference is noted.

| Vaccine | Doses at age (months) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| PCV (PCV13; PCV10 or PCV20 also sold) | 2, 4, 6, booster 12 | 3+1 schedule. A 2022 Ahram article puts the booster at 18 m; the usual labelled window is 12–15 m. Default is 12. A 2+1 schedule (2, 4, 12) is also valid. |
| Rotavirus: Rotarix (default) | 2, 4 | Oral, 2 doses. Dose 1 from 6 weeks. The series must be finished by 24 weeks (about 6 m); never start late. |
| Rotavirus: RotaTeq (alternative) | 2, 4, 6 | Oral, 3 doses, finished by 32 weeks. Record the product given; do not mix product counts. |
| Varicella | 12, 15 | The second dose is at least 3 months after the first (Al Manassa 2026). Some doctors give it at 4–6 y (48–72 m) instead. Supply shortages were reported. |
| Hepatitis A | 12, 18 | Second dose 6–12 months after the first. Ahram 2022 lists 12 m and 24 m. Default is 18; any age from 18–24 is fine. |
| MenACWY conjugate (e.g. Nimenrix) | 12 | Single dose from 12 m. Infant schedules from 6 weeks exist. Ahram 2022 lists 24 m and 4–6 y, which reflects the older polysaccharide practice. Needs doctor confirmation. |
| MenB (Bexsero) | Doctor-led | Not commonly given. Do not auto-schedule; allow manual entry. |
| Influenza (inactivated) | 6, then yearly | In the first season, a child under 9 years gets 2 doses 4 weeks apart, then 1 dose yearly, given before winter (Sep–Nov). |
| Hexavalent | See Table B | This is a route, not an add-on. |
| MMR timing | Government 12, 18 | Some private or foreign-trained doctors use 12 m and 4–6 y. In Egypt, MMR comes from the health office at 12 m and 18 m. Do not add a third MMR. |
| Typhoid conjugate / Vi | 24+ | Travel or at-risk only, not routine. Do not auto-schedule. |
| HPV | Adolescent (doctor-led) | Mentioned as available privately (Al Manassa 2026). Out of scope for the infant schedule. |

## Disagreements and needs doctor confirmation

1. **IPV dose count: 1 or 3.**
   - The Dec 2020 Al-Watan and Dec 2024 Youm7 articles list one IPV dose at 4 m. These articles appear to reuse an older template.
   - The 2021 MoHP letter (Al-Bawaba, Jul 2021), Al-Watan (Apr 2022) and the WHO/UNICEF 2025 revision all say 3 doses at 2/4/6 m since 2021.
   - **Use 3 doses.** This is also the conservative choice. For children born before Feb 2021, 1 dose at 4 m may be on the card. A 2016–2018 birth cohort got 2 fractional doses in catch-up.
2. **Hexavalent in the public schedule.**
   - There is no evidence that it is. It is available only at paid VACSERA and private centres.
   - **Re-check in 2027.** A switch would change Table A, because pentavalent and IPV would become hexavalent.
3. **PCV in the public schedule.** It is not in the schedule yet. The target is "before 2030", tied to local production. Monitor MoHP announcements.
4. **OPV on the hexavalent route.** I assume OPV continues and no source contradicts this. Doctor confirmation is still needed.
5. **PCV booster age.** Ahram 2022 says 18 m and the label says 12–15 m. Use 12 and let the user edit it.
6. **Hep A second dose and MenACWY ages vary.** Ahram 2022 differs from current labelling. These need doctor confirmation.
7. **BCG timing.** Sources say "at birth" or "within the first 15 days". Treat it as birth.
8. **School doses.** MenA+C in grade 1 of each stage is confirmed (Mar 2026). For DT, the grade is unconfirmed. Show school doses as reminders, not as due dates.
9. **The WHO immunizationdata.who.int schedule page did not render** for my fetcher. I could not read its schedule table directly; the WHO data cited here comes from the WUENIC PDF.

## What the app uses (C1, Nov 2026)

Data: `app/lib/features/children/egypt_vaccines.dart`; sources shown in the app: `egypt_vaccine_sources.dart`.

- **Required, government route:** Table A from birth to 18 months (18 doses). School doses (MenA+C, DT) are not scheduled: the app follows children under five.
- **Private hexavalent route:** Table B. Hexavalent 1-3 at 2/4/6 m and a hexavalent booster at 18 m replace pentavalent 1-3, IPV 1-3 and the DTP booster (15 required doses). OPV, BCG, HepB 0 and MMR stay. A DTP booster record counts for the hexavalent booster. On the government route a hexavalent record counts for pentavalent + IPV, so families can switch routes without re-entering doses.
- **Optional (never due or overdue unless the family ticks the vaccine):** PCV 2/4/6/12, Rotarix 2/4, varicella 12/15, hepatitis A 12/18, MenACWY 12, influenza 6/7 (first season only; the yearly autumn dose is not scheduled). RotaTeq, MenB, typhoid and HPV are not scheduled; families can add them as records.
- **MMR:** government 12 and 18 months on both routes.
- **Overdue:** a dose is "due" from two weeks before its date and "overdue" a month after it (unchanged).

**Needs medical review before release:** OPV on the hexavalent route (item 4), the hexavalent booster replacing the DTP booster, the optional defaults (PCV booster age, hepatitis A second dose, MenACWY single dose at 12 m, varicella second dose at 15 m), and the first-season influenza modelling.
